Origins & Vision

Industrializing East Africa

The Dangote East African Refinery is an ambitious private sector industrial development spearheaded by Nigerian businessman Aliko Dangote through the Dangote Group — Africa’s largest diversified manufacturing conglomerate.

Sited at Mokowe, Lamu County, Kenya, the project represents a monumental shift in the continent's energy dynamics. For decades, the East African region has relied almost exclusively on refined fuel imports from the Middle East, India, and Europe, despite crude discoveries across Uganda, Kenya, and South Sudan.

This refinery project bridges the gap by establishing a world-class refining hub on the East African coast, directly connected to the regional logistics networks of Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, and Eastern DR Congo.

About The Dangote Group

Headquartered in Lagos, Nigeria, Dangote Industries Limited is an indigenous conglomerate with operations across 10 African countries. The group has led continent-wide self-sufficiency in cement, sugar, and fertilizer.

Following the commissioning of the world’s largest single-train 650,000 bpd refinery in Lekki, Lagos, the East African venture marks the group's commitment to repeating this transformation across the Indian Ocean maritime corridor.

Geographic Positioning

Why Mokowe, Lamu County?

Lamu's natural maritime topography and national strategic master plans make it the ideal hub for heavy downstream refining.

⚓

Deep Natural Harbor

Lamu possesses natural deep-water channels capable of accommodating Very Large Crude Carriers (VLCCs) and Suezmax tankers without dredging constraints experienced by older regional ports.

🚄

LAPSSET Corridor Nexus

The facility connects directly into the Lamu Port-South Sudan-Ethiopia Transport (LAPSSET) corridor, allowing pipeline, railway, and highway transport deep into landlocked East African markets.

🛠

Ample Industrial Land

Mokowe offers zoned industrial territory necessary for massive buffer zones, auxiliary petrochemical plants, water desalinization units, and captive power generation facilities.

Roadmap & Alignment

Core Strategic Objectives

Fulfilling Kenya's Vision 2030 and East African Community industrial development policies.

1. Regional Energy Security

Protecting East African nations from global geopolitical oil disruptions, tanker freight surges, and foreign exchange shortages caused by massive petroleum import bills.

2. Industrial Value Addition

Transitioning African resources from raw crude exports to high-value refined products, plastics, sulfur for agricultural fertilizers, and petrochemical building blocks.

3. Currency & Reserves Relief

Refined petroleum products currently represent Kenya’s largest foreign exchange drain. Local refining saves billions in hard currency reserves and stabilizes regional exchange rates.

4. High-Skill Job Creation

Generating direct technical roles for process engineers, chemists, marine pilots, and technicians, while training local youth through specialized technical institutes.
